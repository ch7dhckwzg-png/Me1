# Who will actually buy: buyer ranking (23 Nov 2026)

Question from the owner: which of these can afford a website and are likely to pay for one?
Boxing gyms are out. Social 114 (Episode 01) is not ranked here.

## What counts as a likely buyer

1. **Proof they're doing well:** lots of reviews, a team rather than one person, a shopfront on a busy road, and a limited company that files its accounts on time.
2. **High value per customer:** one extra booking pays back a lot (colour work, weddings, clinic treatments, fashion), unlike a £4 coffee.
3. **Signs they already want a site:** a domain bought but never built, or a company set up recently.

## Ranking

1. **The Salon Sanctuary (Liverpool)**: best bet. Active limited company with accounts filed, 456 reviews, two-plus stylists and chair renters, and they **bought thesalonsanctuary.co.uk and .com in 2026 and never built them**. They already want a website; you'd be finishing a job they started.
2. **Mode Beauty and Hair (Pensby, Heswall)**: the busiest business on the list (1,868 reviews), a hair and beauty team on a main road, prices already published. Clearly has the money. Risk: they may feel Fresha already does the job, so the pitch has to be about winning new customers, not bookings.
3. **Golden Afternoon Floristry (Hamilton Square, Birkenhead)**: weddings from £98 up to £6,000. One wedding enquiry from the site pays for it several times over, which makes it the easiest sale to justify on paper.
4. **Goddess Hair & Skin Clinic (Rodney Street, Liverpool)**: Georgian Quarter address, clinic treatments at clinic prices, possibly a second site in Wallasey. High spend per client.
5. **Oxton Boutique (Oxton Village)**: seven years as a limited company with clean filings. A website could sell stock online, which is a bigger job and a bigger fee.
6. **Helen Seaton Hairdressing (Bold Street)**: real intent (company and domain both set up in the last year), but probably one stylist, so a smaller budget.

**Leave out as buyers:** Stollies (company in liquidation), Beth Windsor (mostly solo), HARE (few reviews), Fahy's (no interest in going online), Lane & Brew and YØT (low-margin cafés).

## Before pitching any of them

- Check their Instagram bio and link-in-bio for a website (the Apex lesson).
- Take colours from their real branding.
- A limited company filing accounts on time is good evidence they're organised, not proof of profit. Micro-company accounts don't show turnover.
